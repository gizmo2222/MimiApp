# Game Ideas (not part of MimiApp)

Parked here for now. Move to a dedicated games repo later.

Each idea starts from a game the user likes. The plan is to make original games with similar mechanics, using our own names, art and setting rather than cloning these titles.

## 1. Inspired by *Ultimate Domain* (1993–94 PC strategy game; released in Europe as *Genesia*)
- Core loop: a medieval kingdom builder where you settle new land, grow villages, and expand your territory against rival lords.
- Key mechanics: villages that grow on their own, a production chain (farms, mines, workshops), research and inventions, and a mix of trade and conflict with neighbors.
- Open questions: real-time or turn-based? how much combat vs. economy? map size and number of rivals?

## 2. Inspired by *Black & White* (2001 god game)
- Core loop: you play a god who wins villagers' belief and grows a pet creature that learns from how you reward and punish it.
- Key mechanics: a good/evil alignment that changes the world and visuals, a creature that learns by reinforcement, and gesture-based miracles.
- Open questions: how deep should the creature AI go? scope (3D vs. 2D)?

## 3. Inspired by *A Kingdom for Keflings* (2008 Xbox Live Arcade)
- Core loop: a cozy town builder where you are a giant among tiny villagers, assign them to gather and craft, and combine building pieces into structures.
- Key mechanics: manage workers by picking them up, a resource chain (wood, stone, wool, and so on), and modular building blueprints.
- Open questions: platform? co-op? how much of a story or quest structure?

## 4. Inspired by *Katamari Damacy* (2004 PlayStation 2 game)
- Core loop: roll a sticky ball around a level, picking up objects so the ball grows, letting you pick up bigger and bigger things — from thumbtacks to buildings.
- Key mechanics: grow-to-unlock (size decides what you can grab and where you can go), a time limit, physics-based rolling, and a playful, absurd tone.
- Open questions: our own theme and reason for rolling? 3D scope? levels vs. one big open world?
